# Reanalysis of the results from Corral López et al. (2026)

This repository provides a reanalysis of the results from the paper ‘Imbalance in gut microbial interactions as a marker of health and disease’, published in *Science* by Corral López et al. (2026). 

The original study proposed that an imbalance in microbial interactions, quantified using an ecological index (ENBI), represents a universal signature of disease across multiple gut-related conditions, including inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), irritable bowel syndrome (IBS), colorectal cancer (CRC), and *Clostridioides difficile* infection (CDI). Specifically, the authors reported that diseased microbiomes are characterized by a predominance of positive interactions, whereas healthy microbiomes exhibit more negative interactions.

In this reanalysis, we find that the CRC dataset shows the opposite pattern: healthy samples display higher net interaction values. This result is robust across multiple inference methods and subsampling strategies, and does not support the claim that microbial interaction imbalance constitutes a universal signature of disease.
